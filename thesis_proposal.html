Figure Skating Movement Recognition and Scoring System

Training and optimization based on LRCN model

Research Theme

Figure skating is an ice sport in which athletes use skates to draw figures on the ice and perform difficult movements such as jumping and spinning. Figure skating judges will be based on the quality of the movement and artistic performance of the comprehensive score, is a combination of art and sports. Figure skating has a huge impact around the world and is one of the core sports of the Winter Olympic Games, attracting the attention of a global audience.

In addition to the Olympic Games, figure skating has many international competitions and tournaments, such as the World Figure Skating Championships, European Figure Skating Championships, Four Continents Figure Skating Championships and so on.

Research Focus:This thesis explores the application of Long-term Recurrent Convolutional Networks (LRCN) for automated recognition and scoring of figure skating movements, aiming to create an objective AI judging system.

Important Concept Interpretation

There are three main types of figure skating moves: jump, spin, and step sequence. In the men's and women's singles and pairs competitions, skaters will compete in both the short program and the free skate. Overall, the final score of the contestants is calculated by adding the technical action points and the program content points, and subtracting all the points deducted, and the highest score is the champion.

Jumps

Axel, Lutz, Flip, Loop, Salchow, and Toe Loop jumps with varying degrees of rotation and difficulty.

Spins

Upright spins, sit spins, camel spins, and combination spins with changing positions and variations.

Step Sequences

Intricate patterns of steps, turns, and movements across the ice demonstrating technical skill and edge quality.

Scoring Components

Grade of Execution (GOE), Technical Elements Score (TES), Program Components Score (PCS), and deductions.

Significance and Value

Figure skating is one of the sports with the most complex competition rules and the highest scoring difficulty, and there is currently no mature artificial intelligence model in the world to evaluate and score figure skating movements. Using artificial intelligence model to score can effectively reduce the workload of judges and improve efficiency. And artificial intelligence can ignore distractions such as lights, music, and off-site noise to make the score more objective.

In addition, the movement recognition and intelligent scoring system can also help figure skating beginners to improve their self-practice techniques and movements. The fairness and accuracy of AI are lacking in figure skating competitions at present. Artificial evaluation and scoring tend to have greater errors and even subjective colors. Therefore, the application of AI will make the scoring of competitions more objective and fair, which has high theoretical and practical significance.

Innovative Approach: The LRCN model combines the strengths of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for spatial feature extraction and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for temporal sequence modeling, making it particularly suitable for analyzing the spatiotemporal nature of figure skating movements.

Methodology

The research will utilize a combination of computer vision techniques and deep learning approaches to analyze figure skating performances:

  • Data Collection: Compilation of a comprehensive dataset of figure skating performances from various competitions
  • Preprocessing: Video stabilization, background subtraction, and skater segmentation
  • Feature Extraction: Using CNN architectures to extract spatial features from video frames
  • Temporal Modeling: Applying LSTM networks to model the sequential nature of skating elements
  • Classification & Scoring: Developing algorithms to identify elements and assign scores based on quality metrics
  • Validation: Comparing AI-generated scores with official competition results for accuracy assessment

Expected Outcomes

This research aims to develop a prototype system capable of automatically identifying and evaluating figure skating elements with a target accuracy of 85% or higher when compared to official judging results. The system will provide:

  • Real-time element recognition during performances
  • Technical element score calculations
  • Grade of Execution assessments for each element
  • Detailed feedback on element execution quality
  • Comparative analysis against elite skaters' performances
